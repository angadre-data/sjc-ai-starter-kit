Responsible AI Use

AI can support our work. It does not replace human judgment.

📄
Official SJC AI Policy (2025)

The AI Committee's formal policy for content and media use at SJC Media.

Download PDF View in browser ↗
⚠️
Do not use AI as the final authority

AI should not make final editorial decisions, replace human judgment, or be used for sensitive reporting, opinion, investigative, or culturally sensitive stories.

What AI is good for at SJC

  • Drafting first versions of copy that humans review and revise
  • Summarizing long documents, transcripts, and research materials
  • Generating multiple headline, caption, or subject line options for human selection
  • Reformatting content for different channels (article → social, article → newsletter)
  • Brainstorming story ideas and angles — as inputs to editorial judgment, not replacements for it
  • Transcribing and structuring interview recordings before human review
  • EN/FR translation drafts before bilingual human review
  • Image background removal, upscaling, and editing with appropriate disclosure
  • AI-directed illustrations for editorial packages, fully disclosed
  • Meeting note summarization and action item extraction
  • Analytics interpretation and trend flagging — human confirmation required before acting
  • Generating briefs, proposals, and sales pitch drafts for human review and adaptation
  • SEO metadata generation and content gap analysis
  • Video captioning and social clip formatting

Where human review is always required

👤
Human check required

AI can help draft, summarize, reformat, and suggest. Before using any output, confirm facts, sources, tone, rights, privacy, and policy fit.

  • All published copy — A human must review, edit, and approve every piece of content before it goes live, regardless of how it was drafted.
  • All factual claims — Do not publish AI-generated statistics, quotes, or facts without independent verification. AI can hallucinate plausible-sounding but false information.
  • All quoted sources — Never use AI-generated or AI-paraphrased quotes as if they are direct quotes from a person.
  • All translated content — DeepL and Gemini drafts must be reviewed by a bilingual human before publishing.
  • SEO metadata and headlines — Review for brand voice, accuracy, and editorial standards before applying.
  • AI-generated images — Review for accuracy, appropriateness, and compliance with disclosure requirements before publishing.
  • Analytics recommendations — AI-flagged trends and recommendations require human confirmation before any editorial or business decision is made.
  • Any output going to a client or external audience — Apply additional review and approval steps consistent with your team's existing process.

What we do not use AI for

  • News reporting
  • Opinion writing
  • Investigative journalism
  • Sensitive cultural or community stories
  • Final editorial decision-making
  • Final proofreading (AI is not a reliable proofreader — use human editors)
  • Synthetic voices, deepfakes, or digital replicas of real people
  • Original photographic image creation for covers or primary editorial photography
  • Sponsored or custom content generation where client sensitivity applies — consult your team lead before using AI in branded content workflows

Checking your own AI-assisted work

If you have used AI heavily in a draft and want to check whether it still sounds like your writer, GPTZero (gptzero.me) is a free tool that provides an AI content percentage score. Use it as a signal to iterate — not as a pass/fail gate. A high score means you should revise to ensure the writing reflects the author's voice and judgment. False positives exist: strong human writers can occasionally score as AI.

✅
How to use GPTZero well

Paste your draft. If the AI percentage is high, revise the sections flagged — not to "fool" the detector, but to ensure the copy genuinely reflects human editorial judgment and voice. Then re-read the whole piece as a human editor.

Privacy and data reminders

  • Do not paste confidential source information into any AI tool — including names, identifying details, off-the-record comments, or unpublished story details.
  • Do not paste client data, contracts, or proprietary information into free-tier AI tools (ChatGPT free, Gemini non-Workspace). Use Gemini within Google Workspace where data governance applies.
  • Interview recordings require consent before uploading to transcription tools. Follow your publication's existing consent practices.
  • Employee or candidate data used in recruitment workflows must comply with HR and privacy policies. Check with your HR lead before uploading any candidate information.
  • When in doubt, do not paste it. If you are unsure whether content is safe to share with an AI tool, treat it as confidential and consult your team lead.

Editorial integrity reminders

  • AI-assisted content must meet the same editorial standards as any other content. The tool does not change the standard.
  • Disclosure requirements for AI-generated or AI-assisted content (especially images and video) follow your publication's existing policies. If your publication does not have a policy yet, default to disclosure and consult your editor.
  • AI does not have editorial authority. It does not understand your audience, your brand, your sources, or your community. You do.
  • If an AI output feels wrong — factually, tonally, or ethically — trust that instinct. The human check step is not optional.

When to ask for guidance

  • You are unsure whether a specific AI use case is appropriate for your brand or content type
  • You want to build a new automated or agentic AI workflow that touches published content
  • You are using AI for a consumer-facing product or feature
  • You are using AI in a client or sponsor context
  • You have a privacy or data question about what can be shared with an AI tool

Contact the Data & Analytics Team or raise the question with the AI Committee representative for your brand.
